What this prototype settles, and what it does not

The S-unit tick positions come from measuring the label spacing in the reference photograph. They are not linear in dB: S1 to S9 occupies about 70 % of the sweep for 48 dB, while +10 / +20 / +30 are packed into the remaining 30 % for 30 dB. The spacing also tightens as it climbs within the S-unit region itself. Both are encoded as an anchor table with linear interpolation between anchors, so single marks can be nudged without disturbing the rest.

The reference photograph is taken at an angle, with lamp glare across the face. The anchor values above are good to perhaps a couple of percent of sweep — enough to build against, not enough to cut artwork from. A straight-on scan of the face would let these be tightened.

The SWR marks are not measured from the photograph at all. A moving-coil movement deflects on reflected voltage, so the marks fall where ρ = (SWR−1)/(SWR+1) puts them — 1:1 at rest, 2:1 a third of the way up, 3:1 at half, 5:1 at two thirds, and the scale running to an unreachable open circuit at full deflection. That reproduces the bunching visible at the top of the reference face without any guesswork, and it is what the real instrument is doing.

Detector handling is modelled rather than assumed. A Schottky detector is square-law at low drive and linear at high drive; here that transition is V = Vₖ · x / √(1+x) with x = P/Pₖ, which is invertible in closed form. Turning the linearisation off in the panel above shows the reading the firmware would produce if it assumed a plain square-root law throughout — correct near full power, badly wrong at low drive, which is exactly when someone is tuning up.

Not yet addressed: display hardware and rendering target are still open, so nothing here commits to a panel size or an interface. The face artwork is drawn to a canvas at device resolution and the geometry is parameterised, so it can be re-scaled once that decision lands.
