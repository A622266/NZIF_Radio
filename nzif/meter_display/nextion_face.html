What this panel changes

The Nextion is not a framebuffer. It runs its own firmware and its own 48 MHz MCU, holds the artwork in its 16 MB of flash, and takes drawing instructions over UART. The Pico never sends pixels — it sends a needle angle. That suits meter.c exactly, since it already emits a sweep fraction and draws nothing.

The one thing that does not survive contact with this panel is the built-in Gauge component. It repaints its whole bounding square every update — on the order of 20,000 pixels — which both flickers and throttles the update rate. The needle is also a plain straight line, so the tapered Ten-Tec needle is not available through it.

The workable method is to draw the needle with line and undraw it by restoring the background underneath. That is cheap only if the restore is cheap, which is what the swept-band option above is for: over a flat colour you can undraw with a second line in the background colour, roughly 140 pixels a frame. Over the amber gradient and the scale printing you cannot, and you need xpic to put the artwork back from the stored picture — correct, but a rectangle at a time.

Splitting the needle into short segments keeps those rectangles small: the cost table above compares the options as you change the face. The trade is honest and visual — a flat band under the needle is cheaper and faster, and costs you the lamp glow exactly where the eye is looking.
